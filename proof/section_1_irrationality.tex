\begin{theorem}
The number $\sqrt{7}$ is irrational.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We argue by contradiction. The proof will avoid assuming a reduced fraction
representation, and we will prove explicitly the divisibility fact that is needed.

First, we prove the following elementary lemma.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Lemma.} If $n$ is an integer and $7 \mid n^2$, then $7 \mid n$.

\smallskip

\noindent\textit{Proof of the lemma.}
By the division algorithm, every integer $n$ can be written as
\[
n=7t+r
\]
for some integers $t$ and $r$ with
\[
r \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}.
\]
Then
\[
n^2 \equiv r^2 \pmod 7.
\]
The possible values of $r^2$ modulo $7$ are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
r & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \\ \hline
r^2 \bmod 7 & 0 & 1 & 4 & 2 & 2 & 4 & 1
\end{array}
\]
Thus $r^2 \equiv 0 \pmod 7$ only when $r=0$. Therefore, if
\[
7 \mid n^2,
\]
then $n^2 \equiv 0 \pmod 7$, so $r=0$, and hence
\[
n=7t.
\]
So $7 \mid n$. This proves the lemma.

\medskip

Now suppose, for contradiction, that $\sqrt{7}$ is rational. Then there exist integers
$a$ and $b$ with $b \neq 0$ such that
\[
\sqrt{7}=\frac{a}{b}.
\]
Changing the signs of both $a$ and $b$ if necessary, we may assume $b>0$.

Consider the set
\[
S=\left\{ b \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0} : \text{there exists } a \in \mathbb{Z}
\text{ such that } \sqrt{7}=\frac{a}{b} \right\}.
\]
This set is nonempty because $\sqrt{7}$ is assumed rational. By the well-ordering
principle, $S$ has a least element. Choose $b \in S$ minimal, and choose an integer
$a$ such that
\[
\sqrt{7}=\frac{a}{b}.
\]

Squaring both sides gives
\[
7=\frac{a^2}{b^2}.
\]
Since $b \neq 0$, multiplying both sides by $b^2$ gives
\[
a^2=7b^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
7 \mid a^2.
\]
By the lemma, $7 \mid a$, so there exists an integer $c$ such that
\[
a=7c.
\]
Substituting this into $a^2=7b^2$, we get
\[
(7c)^2=7b^2.
\]
Hence
\[
49c^2=7b^2.
\]
Dividing both sides by $7$ gives
\[
7c^2=b^2.
\]
Thus
\[
7 \mid b^2.
\]
By the lemma again, $7 \mid b$, so there exists an integer $d$ such that
\[
b=7d.
\]
Since $b>0$, we have $d>0$, and since $7>1$,
\[
d=\frac{b}{7}<b.
\]

Now
\[
\sqrt{7}=\frac{a}{b}=\frac{7c}{7d}=\frac{c}{d}.
\]
Thus $d \in S$. But $d<b$, contradicting the minimality of $b$ in $S$.

Therefore our assumption that $\sqrt{7}$ is rational was false. Hence
$\sqrt{7}$ is irrational.
\end{proof}
