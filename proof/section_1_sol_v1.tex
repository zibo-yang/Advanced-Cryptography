**Claim:** $\sqrt{7}$ is irrational.

**Proof.** I will use **Euclid’s lemma**: if $p$ is prime and $p \mid xy$, then $p \mid x$ or $p \mid y$. In particular, if $p \mid x^2$, then $p \mid x$. ([proofwiki.org](https://proofwiki.org/wiki/Euclid%27s_Lemma_for_Prime_Divisors?utm_source=openai))

Assume, for contradiction, that $\sqrt{7}$ is rational. Then there exist integers $a$ and $b$ with $b \neq 0$ and $\gcd(a,b)=1$ such that

\[
\sqrt{7}=\frac{a}{b}.
\]

Squaring both sides gives

\[
7=\frac{a^2}{b^2},
\]

so

\[
a^2=7b^2.
\]

Thus $7 \mid a^2$. Since $7$ is prime, Euclid’s lemma implies that $7 \mid a$. Therefore, for some integer $k$,

\[
a=7k.
\]

Substitute this into $a^2=7b^2$:

\[
(7k)^2=7b^2.
\]

Hence

\[
49k^2=7b^2.
\]

Dividing both sides by $7$ gives

\[
7k^2=b^2.
\]

So $7 \mid b^2$. Again, since $7$ is prime, Euclid’s lemma implies that $7 \mid b$.

We have shown that $7 \mid a$ and $7 \mid b$, contradicting $\gcd(a,b)=1$. Therefore the assumption that $\sqrt{7}$ is rational is false.

Hence,

\[
\boxed{\sqrt{7}\text{ is irrational}.}
\]
