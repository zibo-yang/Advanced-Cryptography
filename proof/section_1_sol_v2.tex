\section*{Proof}

We prove that $\sqrt{7}$ is irrational.

First, we establish a lemma.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Lemma.} For every integer $n$, if $7 \mid n^2$, then $7 \mid n$.

\begin{proof}
Let $n$ be any integer. By the division algorithm, there exist integers $a$ and $r$ such that
\[
n=7a+r
\]
with
\[
r \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}.
\]
Then
\[
n^2=(7a+r)^2=49a^2+14ar+r^2=7(7a^2+2ar)+r^2.
\]
Hence
\[
n^2-r^2=7(7a^2+2ar),
\]
so
\[
7 \mid (n^2-r^2).
\]

Now suppose $7 \mid n^2$. Since $7 \mid n^2$ and $7 \mid (n^2-r^2)$, it follows that
\[
7 \mid r^2.
\]
But for the possible values of $r$, we have
\[
0^2=0,\quad 1^2=1,\quad 2^2=4,\quad 3^2=9,\quad 4^2=16,\quad 5^2=25,\quad 6^2=36.
\]
Among these numbers, the only one divisible by $7$ is $0$. Therefore
\[
r=0.
\]
Thus
\[
n=7a,
\]
so
\[
7 \mid n.
\]
This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\medskip

Now suppose, for contradiction, that $\sqrt{7}$ is rational. Then there exist integers $m$ and $n$ with $n \neq 0$ such that
\[
\sqrt{7}=\frac{m}{n}.
\]
Replacing $n$ by $-n$ and $m$ by $-m$ if necessary, we may assume
\[
n>0.
\]

Among all positive denominators of fractions representing $\sqrt{7}$, choose the smallest one. This is possible by the well-ordering principle, since the set of such positive denominators is a nonempty subset of the positive integers. Thus choose integers $p$ and $q$ such that
\[
q>0,\qquad \sqrt{7}=\frac{p}{q},
\]
and such that $q$ is as small as possible among all positive denominators representing $\sqrt{7}$.

Squaring both sides gives
\[
7=\frac{p^2}{q^2}.
\]
Multiplying by $q^2$ gives
\[
p^2=7q^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
7 \mid p^2.
\]
By the lemma, applied to the integer $p$, we get
\[
7 \mid p.
\]
Hence there exists an integer $k$ such that
\[
p=7k.
\]
Substituting this into $p^2=7q^2$, we obtain
\[
(7k)^2=7q^2.
\]
Thus
\[
49k^2=7q^2.
\]
Dividing by $7$ gives
\[
7k^2=q^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
7 \mid q^2.
\]
By the lemma, applied now to the integer $q$, we get
\[
7 \mid q.
\]
Hence there exists an integer $\ell$ such that
\[
q=7\ell.
\]
Since $q>0$, we have $\ell>0$. Also, because $q=7\ell$, we have
\[
\ell=\frac{q}{7}<q.
\]

Now
\[
\sqrt{7}=\frac{p}{q}=\frac{7k}{7\ell}=\frac{k}{\ell}.
\]
This represents $\sqrt{7}$ with positive denominator $\ell$, and
\[
0<\ell<q.
\]
This contradicts the choice of $q$ as the smallest positive denominator of any fraction representing $\sqrt{7}$.

Therefore our assumption that $\sqrt{7}$ is rational was false. Hence
\[
\sqrt{7}
\]
is irrational.
