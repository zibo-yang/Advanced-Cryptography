--- ISSUES ---

Issue 1
Anchor: global
Location: "and we may choose such a representation with $\gcd(p,q)=1$."
Description: Justification Gap — The proof assumes without justification that a rational
number can always be represented by integers p and q with gcd(p,q)=1. This reduced-form
existence theorem (every rational has a representation in lowest terms) is required for
the final contradiction but is never stated or proved.

Issue 2
Anchor: global
Location: "Therefore, the only way for $p^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$ is for $p \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$."
Description: Justification Gap — The inference from the exhaustive table of squared
residues to the conclusion relies on two unstated modular-arithmetic facts: (a) every
integer is congruent modulo 7 to exactly one of 0,1,2,3,4,5,6, and (b) squaring
preserves congruence classes modulo 7. Neither fact is explicitly justified in the proof.

Issue 3
Anchor: global
Location: "Therefore $7 \mid q^2$. By the same argument as before, this implies $7 \mid q$."
Description: Justification Gap — The earlier residue-table argument was stated specifically
about p. Applying it to q by appeal to "the same argument as before" is informal and
incomplete: the proof never establishes the result as a general lemma (e.g., "for any
integer n, if 7 | n^2 then 7 | n"). Without such a general statement, re-use of the
argument requires re-verification for q, which the proof omits.
