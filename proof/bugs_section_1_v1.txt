=== ISSUES LIST ===

[Run 1] Justification Gap — Location: initial assumption of coprime representation.
  The proof asserts the existence of a coprime integer representation (gcd(a,b)=1) from
  rationality without explicitly stating or justifying the reduced-fraction theorem.
  Rationality alone gives a/b with b≠0; the additional condition gcd(a,b)=1 requires
  a separate reduction-to-lowest-terms argument.

[Run 2] Justification Gap — Location: initial assumption of coprime representation.
  The proof assumes without justification that a rational number can be represented as
  a fraction of coprime integers. The definition of rationality only gives integers a,b
  with b≠0; obtaining gcd(a,b)=1 requires an additional lowest-terms argument or theorem,
  which is not stated.

[Run 3, Issue 1] Justification Gap — Location: initial assumption of coprime representation.
  The proof assumes without justification that any rational number can be represented as a
  fraction of integers in lowest terms. The existence of a,b with gcd(a,b)=1 requires an
  explicitly stated reduction-to-lowest-terms theorem or argument.

[Run 3, Issue 2] Justification Gap — Location: final contradiction step.
  The contradiction relies on the unstated fact that if an integer greater than 1 divides
  both a and b, then gcd(a,b)≠1. The proof does not explicitly connect "7∣a and 7∣b" to
  the claimed contradiction with gcd(a,b)=1.

=== SUMMARY OF DISTINCT ISSUES ===

Issue A (flagged by all 3 runs): The move from "sqrt(7) is rational" to "there exist a,b
  with gcd(a,b)=1 and sqrt(7)=a/b" is unjustified. The proof silently invokes the
  fundamental theorem that every rational number has a representation in lowest terms,
  but never states or proves this reduction step.

Issue B (flagged by run 3 only): The final contradiction step — "7∣a and 7∣b contradicts
  gcd(a,b)=1" — is stated without justification. The logical connection between a common
  divisor greater than 1 and the condition gcd(a,b)=1 is assumed but not established.
